Soccer's rules are called the Laws of the Game. There are 17 of them, written by a group called the IFAB. Youth leagues change a few to fit kids. Here are the big ones.

The goal

Get the ball all the way over the other team's goal line, between the posts and under the crossbar. Most goals wins. A 9v9 youth game is usually two 30-minute halves.

No hands!

Field players can use feet, legs, chest and head, but not hands or arms. Touching it on purpose with your hand or arm is a handball. Only the goalkeeper can use hands, and only inside their own penalty area.

Ball out of play

  • Over the sideline: throw-in for the team that didn't touch it last. Both hands, ball from behind your head, both feet on the ground.
  • Over the goal line, last touched by attackers: goal kick.
  • Over the goal line, last touched by defenders: corner kick.

Fouls & free kicks

Tripping, pushing, kicking or holding an opponent is a foul. The other team gets a free kick, and opponents must back up. A foul by defenders inside their own penalty area gives a penalty kick: one shooter against the keeper.

Yellow & red cards

A yellow card is a warning for bad behavior or a reckless foul. Two yellows, or one really serious foul, means a red card: you have to leave the game and your team plays one short.

Offside (the tricky one)

When a teammate passes to you, you're offside if you were closer to the other goal than both the ball and the second-to-last defender (the keeper usually counts as one). It only counts in the other team's half, and never from a throw-in, goal kick or corner. 9v9 games use offside.

Heading

To protect young brains, U.S. Soccer says no heading at all for 11 and under, in games or practice. At U12, heading practice is limited. If the ball is in the air, use your chest or let it bounce.

New for 2026 Hurry up!

  • If a team takes too long on a throw-in or goal kick, the referee can start a 5-second countdown. Too slow? Throw-in goes to the other team; goal kick becomes a corner for the other team.
  • Goalkeepers can only hold the ball about 8 seconds, or the other team gets a corner.
  • A player getting help for an injury on the field must stay off for 1 minute.

These are the newest pro rules. Your league may not use all of them yet.

Good sports

  • Shin guards every time, socks pulled over them
  • Everyone plays goalkeeper sometimes until U11
  • Help a player up after a fall
  • Shake hands with the other team and thank the ref

Shape = triangles

Look at the dots: players stand in triangles, never in a straight line. A triangle gives the ball carrier two passing options at once.

First touch

Your first touch should push the ball away from pressure and into space, not stop it dead at your feet. Cushion it with the inside of your foot, like catching an egg.

Passing

  • Plant foot next to the ball, toes pointing at your target
  • Strike with the inside of the foot, ankle locked
  • Follow through toward your teammate
  • Pass to the foot away from their defender

Dribbling

Lots of small touches with both feet, ball close, head up between touches so you can see. Speed up after you beat a defender.

Shooting

  • Strike with your laces, toe pointed down
  • Body over the ball keeps it low
  • Aim for the low corners: hardest for keepers
  • Placement beats power

Defending

  • Stay goal-side: between your player and your goal
  • Low, bent knees, body sideways
  • Don't dive in. Be patient, then poke the ball when it's loose
  • Push them toward the sideline

Goalkeeping

  • Ready stance: on your toes, hands up
  • Make a "W" with your hands for high balls
  • Get your body behind the ball
  • After a save, start the attack fast

Pro academies don't make kids stand in lines anymore. The newest coaching is all about playing game-like situations and making decisions. Here's how the pros train, and a game to try it.

U.S. Soccer

Play-Practice-Play

Practice starts with a small game, then a focused activity on one skill, then a big game at the end. Less standing around, more touches, and you figure out answers yourself.

More touches

Small-sided games

3v3, 4v4, 5v5. Fewer players means you touch the ball way more and make more decisions every minute.

Barcelona style

Rondos

Keep-away in a circle: passers on the outside, defenders in the middle. It trains quick passing, angles and thinking ahead. Try it below!

Pro secret

Scanning

The best players look over their shoulder before the ball arrives, so they already know where defenders and teammates are. Studies show scanning can be trained, and it helps decision-making.

Game brain

Decisions over drills

Sports scientists found kids learn better in game-like practice (with defenders and choices) than from repeating the same drill. That's why this academy has you play, not just read.

Talk

Use your voice

  • "Man on!" a defender is coming
  • "Time!" no one's near, take a touch
  • "Turn!" you have space to face forward
  • "Switch!" pass to the other side

10-minute home ball mastery

Do this in the yard or garage, 4 or 5 days a week. Both feet every time.

  • 1 min Toe taps: tap the top of the ball, switching feet fast
  • 1 min Inside-inside: tap it side to side between your feet
  • 1 min Sole rolls: roll it across your body with the bottom of your foot
  • 2 min Wall passes: pass against a wall, control, pass again. Left foot too!
  • 2 min Cone dribble: weave through 5 cones (or shoes)
  • 2 min Moves: practice 1 move from the Skills tab, both directions
  • 1 min Juggling: count your record
