Flag football is usually played 5 on 5 on a smaller field than tackle football. A common youth field (NFL FLAG) is about 30 yards wide and 70 yards long, with two 10-yard end zones. Leagues can vary a little. Tap the parts of the field.

These are the official NFL FLAG rules most youth leagues use. Your league might change a few, so always listen to your coach and the ref.

No contact

No tackling, blocking or pushing. Instead of a tackle, you pull a flag off the ball carrier's belt. Flag pulled = play over.

Downs

Your team starts on its own 5-yard line. You get 4 downs (tries) to cross midfield. Once you cross, you get 3 downs to score a touchdown. Don't make it? The other team gets the ball.

The quarterback

  • The QB has 7 seconds to throw (the pass clock)
  • The QB can't run with the ball past the line, unless they hand it off first and get it back
  • Only one forward pass per play

Rushing the QB

Defenders can rush the quarterback only if they start 7 yards back from the line of scrimmage (the "rush line"). If the ball is handed off, anyone can rush.

No-run zones

5 yards before each end zone and 5 yards on each side of midfield. Inside them you must pass to get a first down or a touchdown. No handoffs or runs!

Scoring

  • Touchdown: 6 points
  • Extra point: after a TD, try for 1 point from the 5-yard line (pass only) or 2 points from the 10
  • Safety: 2 points for the defense if the ball carrier is stopped in their own end zone
  • Interceptions can be run back for a score

Flag guarding

It's illegal to protect your flags with your hand, arm or the ball: no stiff-arms, no lowering your elbow, no covering your belt. That's a penalty.

Good sports

  • Everyone on offense can catch a pass
  • Shirts tucked in so the flags hang free
  • Shake hands after every game

Man-to-man defense

Each defender sticks to one receiver wherever they go. Look for defenders lined up right across from receivers. Beat it with quick cuts: slants, outs, crossing routes.

Zone defense

Each defender guards an area, not a person. Look for defenders spread out, away from receivers. Beat it with routes that stop in the empty spaces (curls) or flood one area with two receivers.

Receivers run planned paths called routes. Together they make the "route tree." Tap a route to watch a receiver run it.
