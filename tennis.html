Every line on the court has a job.

Is it in?

If any part of the ball touches the line, it's in. In matches without umpires, you call the lines on your side, and if you're not sure, it's in.

Serving rules

  • Stand behind the baseline, on one side of the center mark
  • Serve diagonally into the box across the net
  • You get 2 tries. Miss one = fault. Miss both = double fault (you lose the point)
  • Ball touches the net but lands in? That's a let: serve again
  • Stepping on the baseline before you hit = foot fault

Kid-size courts

Younger players use smaller courts: a 36-foot court with red balls, then a 60-foot court with orange balls, then the full court with green balls. Around age 10, most kids move to the green ball on the big court.

  • The server's score is said first: "30–15" means the server has 30.
  • Tied at 40–40 is deuce. Win the next point for advantage; win again to take the game. Lose it and it's back to deuce.
  • A set is won by the first to 6 games, ahead by 2. At 6–6, play a tiebreak: first to 7 points, ahead by 2 (counted 1, 2, 3…).
  • Players switch serving every game and change ends after odd games (1, 3, 5…).
  • Most matches are best of 3 sets. Kids' events often use shorter sets (first to 4 games).

Grips: how you hold the racquet

Continental

"Hold it like a hammer." Put the racquet's edge down and shake hands with the handle.

Use it for: serves, volleys, overheads, slices.

Eastern forehand

"Shake hands with the racquet." Strings face the side; your palm is flat behind the handle.

Use it for: flat, solid forehands. Great first forehand grip.

Semi-western

Turn your hand a little further under the handle than Eastern.

Use it for: topspin forehands, which most pros use today.

Two-handed backhand

Bottom hand in continental, top hand in an eastern forehand grip, hands touching.

Most kids learn the two-hander first: it's stronger and steadier.

Footwork: the secret weapon

  • Ready position: feet wide, knees bent, racquet in front, weight on the balls of your feet.
  • Split step: a little hop right as your opponent hits. It wakes up your legs so you can push off in any direction.
  • Unit turn: turn your shoulders and hips together as soon as you see which side the ball is going.
  • Recover: after every shot, move back toward the middle of the baseline.

Shoes & extras

  • Tennis shoes: flat soles and strong sides for quick side-to-side moves. Running shoes are built for going forward and can roll.
  • Overgrip: a thin wrap on the handle for better grip. Replace it when it gets slick.
  • Dampener: a little rubber piece in the strings that quiets the "ping."
  • Water, a hat and sunscreen for outdoor courts.

Court manners

  • Wait for a point to end before walking behind another court
  • Roll stray balls back between points, not during
  • Server announces the score before each point
  • Shake hands at the net after the match
