Check

The king is being attacked. You must get out of check on your very next move: move the king, block, or capture the attacker.

Checkmate

The king is in check and there is no way out. Game over, you win! (You never actually capture the king.)

Stalemate

Not in check, but no legal move at all. That's a draw, not a win. So when you're way ahead, always leave the enemy king a move!

Top kids' chess coaches teach the same way: learn one piece at a time with mini-games, then solve puzzles every day, then play and review real games. Here are the rules they drill into every new player.

Before every move: CCT

Look for Checks, Captures and Threats, for you and your opponent. Ask: "What does their last move want to do?"

Opening: the first 10 moves

  • Take the center with a pawn (e4 or d4)
  • Bring out knights and bishops
  • Castle early to keep your king safe
  • Don't bring the queen out too soon
  • Don't move the same piece twice without a reason
  • Connect your rooks

Count the points

Pawn 1 · Knight 3 · Bishop 3 · Rook 5 · Queen 9. Only trade when you get equal or more points back. If you're ahead, trading pieces makes winning easier.

Knight on the rim is dim

A knight in the middle can reach 8 squares. In a corner it reaches only 2. Keep knights near the center.

Tournament manners

  • Touch-move: touch a piece, you must move it
  • Shake hands before and after
  • Say "good game," win or lose
  • Most games use a chess clock: hit it with the same hand you moved with

How players improve

  • Solve a few puzzles every day
  • Play slower games, not just speed chess
  • Go over your losses: find the move that went wrong
  • Learn the basic checkmates: queen + king, and rook + king

Words to know

  • Fork: one piece attacks two at once (knights love forks)
  • Pin: a piece can't move without exposing something bigger behind it
  • Skewer: a pin in reverse: the big piece is in front
  • Develop: bring pieces off the back row
  • Blunder: a big mistake

Reading a chess move

Columns are files a–h. Rows are ranks 1–8. Every square has a name like e4. Pieces use letters: K king, Q queen, R rook, B bishop, N knight (K was taken!). Nf3 = knight to f3. x = capture, + = check, # = checkmate, O-O = castle.
