Parts of the weapon

Coach says: Parry with your forte (the strong part near the guard) and hit with your tip. The bell guard protects your hand.

There are three fencing weapons. Each one has its own target area (where a hit counts) and its own rules. Most kids start with foil.

Right of way (foil and sabre)

If both fencers hit at about the same time, the referee decides who started the attack first. The attacker has "right of way." To take it away, the defender must parry (block) or make the attacker miss. Then the defender's riposte has right of way.

Épée has no right of way: whoever hits first scores. If both hit within 1/25 of a second, both get a point (a "double").

The scoring box

Red = left fencer scored Green = right fencer scored White = off-target (foil only)

The fencers are wired up with body cords. When a tip presses in, or a sabre touches the metallic jacket, the box lights up.

Fencing happens on a long, narrow strip called the piste (say "peest"). Tap the parts to learn them.

How a bout goes

  • Salute your opponent, the referee, and the crowd with your weapon
  • Masks on. Referee: "En garde! Prêts? Allez!"
  • After each touch the referee says "Halte!" and you go back to the en garde lines
  • Pool bouts: first to 5 touches (3 minutes)
  • Direct elimination bouts: usually to 15 (three 3-minute periods). Younger age groups sometimes fence shorter bouts.
  • At the end: salute, take off your mask, and shake hands with your non-weapon hand

Penalty cards

  • Yellow: a warning
  • Red: a touch is given to your opponent
  • Black: you're out of the event (like refusing to salute or shake hands)

En garde stance

  • Feet at a right angle, about shoulder-width apart
  • Front foot points at your opponent
  • Knees bent, weight in the middle
  • Weapon arm bent, tip pointing at the opponent
  • Back arm up (foil and épée) or on your hip (sabre)
